App Relajante: Relaxation booking

A mobile app for booking massage and relaxation sessions, designed end to end using the five stages of design thinking.

Mobile app

Context

A new way to book relaxation

The app was designed to simplify how people discover and book relaxation services such as massages. Users struggled to manage appointments across different platforms, leading to friction and drop-off before booking was complete.

The project applied design thinking end to end, from empathising with users' pain points through to testing a working MVP.

Problem

Pain points and solutions

Research surfaced two consistent friction areas that the design needed to resolve.

Pain point

Booking a session is time-consuming and fragmented

Users had to search across multiple channels, contact businesses via WhatsApp, and wait for a reply before confirming a time. The process often took hours.

Solution

Instant booking with real-time availability

A unified calendar where users select date, time slot, and confirm in under a minute, with automatic push reminders before the session.

Pain point

No history or accountability after the session

Users had no record of past sessions, making it difficult to track providers, frequency, or preferences over time.

Solution

Session history with status tracking

A history tab showing all bookings with their status (Confirmado, Cancelado, Finalizado) and the ability to rebook in one tap.

Research

Who books relaxation services

Two distinct personas emerged from the research phase, guiding every design decision from navigation to copy tone.

Juan Carlos

Wellness seeker

A person motivated to incorporate relaxation into his weekly routine. He already knows massage therapy is beneficial and wants a fast, frictionless way to schedule sessions without negotiating times via chat.

Relationship with the app

Uses bookings and history tabs weekly. Especially values the session reminder feature that helps him stay consistent.

Magali

Occasional user

A curious person who tries relaxation services occasionally but hasn't made it a habit. She's interested in the experience and likes discovering new service types, but needs a low-friction entry point to try something new.

Relationship with the app

Explores service listings, books sporadically. Values ease of sign-up and not needing to commit to a package upfront.

Final solution

A booking flow that gets out of the way

The final screens centre around two flows: booking a new session and reviewing past ones. The design keeps both paths minimal, using progressive disclosure to surface detail only when needed.

Full design presentation: methodology, personas, userflow, wireframes, voice and tone, and testing results.

Testing

What the MVP revealed

Usability testing was conducted with real users on the MVP prototype. The main finding was that users understood the booking flow immediately but needed clearer confirmation feedback after completing a booking. A success state screen was added in the iteration.

Voice and tone testing showed that the bilingual copy (Spanish / English taglines) resonated positively, reinforcing the modern and approachable feel the app aimed for.
